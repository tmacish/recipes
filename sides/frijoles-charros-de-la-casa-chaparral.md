# Frijoles Charros de la Casa Chaparral

### *(or: beans)*

Pinto beans cooked from dry, then finished in a pot of rendered bacon, onion, jalapeño, tomato and chipotle. No ham. The bacon does that job on its own and the beans do not need two kinds of pork arguing in the same pot. Cook the beans plain first and build the flavor around them afterward, which keeps the beans tasting like beans instead of like a seasoning packet.

**Serves:** 8
**Prep:** 10 minutes · **Cook:** 2 1/2 hours

## Ingredients

### Cooking the beans
- 1 lb dried pinto beans
- 1/2 yellow onion, left in one piece
- 2 garlic cloves, smashed
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp Mexican oregano
- Water to cover by 3 inches

### The pot
- 8 oz bacon, diced
- 1/2 yellow onion, diced
- 1 jalapeño, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 3 Roma tomatoes, diced
- 1 chipotle in adobo, minced, plus a little of the sauce
- 1/2 tsp ground cumin
- Kosher salt, to taste
- Cilantro, chopped, to finish

## Method

1. **Sort and rinse.** Tip the beans onto a sheet pan and pick out the shriveled ones, the split ones, and the small rock that is always in there. Rinse and drain.

2. **Cook them plain.** Beans, onion half, smashed garlic, bay leaves, salt and oregano into a large pot or Dutch oven. Cover by three inches of water. Bring to a boil, drop to a bare simmer, cover, and cook 1 1/2 to 2 hours until a bean crushes easily against the roof of your mouth. Top up with hot water if the level drops below the beans.

3. **Drain, and keep the liquid.** This is the part people throw away and regret. Strain the beans, saving every drop of the cooking liquid. Fish out the bay leaves, onion and garlic. Measure out 4 cups of liquid, topping up with water or chicken broth if you are short.

4. **Render the bacon.** Same pot, no need to wash it. Bacon over medium heat for about 8 minutes, until the edges crisp and the fat is out. Leave the fat in the pot.

5. **Onion and jalapeño.** In they go, about 5 minutes, until the onion turns translucent and the pepper softens.

6. **Garlic, 30 seconds.** Just until it smells like something. Any longer over that much hot fat and it turns bitter.

7. **Tomatoes, chipotle, cumin.** Cook 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until the tomatoes slump and give up their juice and the whole thing goes from chopped vegetables to a base.

8. **Reunite.** Beans back in with the 4 cups of reserved liquid. Bring to a boil, then taste. This is the only place salt matters, since the beans were salted once already and the bacon brought its own.

9. **Finish with cilantro** and serve. They should be soupy, closer to a bowl of beans in broth than a side of refried.

## Notes

- **Where it lands.** Mild on purpose. One jalapeño and one chipotle in eight servings is background warmth, not heat, which is what you want from something sitting next to a pepper-heavy brisket. To push it, leave the jalapeño seeds in, add a second chipotle, or stir in a spoon of the adobo at the end.
- **No ham, and what that costs.** The original build uses cubed ham alongside the bacon. Dropping it loses a little sweetness and a little body, and gains a pot that tastes of one pork instead of two. The bacon fat is carrying the whole thing either way, so do not skimp there.
- **Soaking is optional.** Unsoaked beans take longer and taste the same. If you do soak overnight, start checking for tenderness at the 45 minute mark instead of 90.
- **Salt early, despite what you have heard.** The old rule about salt toughening bean skins does not hold up. Salting the cooking water seasons the bean itself, which you cannot do later no matter how much you add to the pot.
- **The canned shortcut.** Four 15 oz cans of pintos, drained and rinsed, plus 4 cups chicken broth in place of the bean liquid. Start at step 4. Worse, but on a weeknight it is a real option.
- **Better the next day.** Like chili, these improve overnight. Make them up to two days ahead. They keep a week in the fridge and freeze for 3 months.
- **The name.** The Chaparral Apartments were complex out near 50th and Slide in Lubbock, with three roommates in it and nothing grand about it whatsoever. I ate a lot of beans there.
